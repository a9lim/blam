# Blc — Lean 4 formalization

The kernel-checked BLC formalization behind blam's certificate layer. No
mathlib and no `sorry`; every theorem depends at most on `propext` and
`Quot.sound`.

```
theorem loop32_noNormalForm : ¬ HasNormalForm loop32     -- axioms: propext
theorem headDiverges_not_hasNormalForm :
    HeadDiverges t → ¬ HasNormalForm t                   -- propext, Quot.sound
theorem loop32_headDiverges : HeadDiverges loop32        -- propext, Quot.sound
theorem RatchetCert.noNormalForm :
    c.Valid → ¬ HasNormalForm c.T                        -- propext, Quot.sound
theorem argKill : headSteps k t = some s → headStep s = none →
    hnfArgB a s = true → ¬ HasNormalForm a →
    ¬ HasNormalForm t                                    -- propext, Quot.sound
theorem selfint_contract : Closed M →
    Betas (intL C (streamOf (blcCode M) N)) (C (λ M) N)  -- propext, Quot.sound
theorem parserStatement : ParserStatement               -- propext, Quot.sound
theorem selfint_contract165 : Closed M →
    Betas (intL165 C (streamOf (blcCode M) N)) (C (λ M) N)
                                                         -- propext, Quot.sound
theorem not_selfintSizeBound_166 : ¬ SelfintSizeBound 166
                                                         -- propext, Quot.sound
```

The 32-bit term that the reference busy-beaver ledger excludes by hand has no
normal form under arbitrary β-reduction. Palmer's 168-bit `intL` (pinned to its
wire by `wire_intL`) is a self-interpreter: for every closed `M` and any terms
`C`, `N`, `intL C (⌜M⌝ ++ N)` β-reduces to `C (λz. M) N`. So is the 165-bit
`intL165` (`wire_intL165`), so no self-interpreter size bound above 165
holds. The **general bridge** holds for
every term, so any head-divergence certificate concludes no-normal-form with
no side conditions. The **class assemblies** turn certificate *data* into that
conclusion mechanically, and the **rigid-head argument bridge** lifts a spine
argument's divergence to the whole term.

## Generated certificates

`Certs/` holds **305 kernel-checked `¬HasNormalForm` theorems**, one per line
of `data/certificates/ratchet_kills.tsv`, emitted by the untrusted
`blam cert lean`: 222 `RatchetCert`, 35 `HTRCert`, 40 `SelCert`, and 8
`PdrCert` literals, one module per term size. The ten `*-ARG` lines (8/1/1/0
by class) reach their target through `argKill` on top of the inner assembly.
The kernel replays each certificate obligation by obligation (`by decide`),
and each carries a `wire_*` theorem pinning the certified term to the bits in
its name.

`BusyBeaver.lean` is a second registry, generated by
`tools/certificates/bb.py` from `data/certificates/bb_seeds.tsv`,
`bb_halt_seeds.tsv`, and the matching census lambda wraps: **110 exclusions
and eight exact halting outputs** from the BB(33/34/35) proofs. Every entry
pins its term to literal wire bits and proves closedness; divergence entries
prove no normal form, halting entries prove existence and the exact size of
every normal form. `bb.py --check` audits the aggregate axioms and compares
the generated files against the seed ledgers. Together with the exhaustive
Rust census these close BBλ(33), BBλ(34), and BBλ(35); the enumeration and the
halting engine are not formalized (`docs/classical/certificates/busy-beaver.md`).

## Two routes to loop32

1. **The one-way street** (`Blc/NoNf.lean`): loop32 needs no
   standardization. A, F, C0 and every tower Wⁿ[C0] are β-normal, so every
   reachable state carries exactly one redex and β-reduction from loop32 is
   deterministic: an invariant family closed under β whose members always
   step.
2. **Head factorization** (`Blc/Subst.lean`, `Blc/Par.lean`,
   `Blc/Factor.lean`): the Accattoli–Faggian–Guerrieri indexed route.
   Parallel reduction is indexed by contracted-redex count. The split
   exposes at most one head step per application with the index strictly
   dropping, so no head chain is ever absorbed into a parallel step (the
   failure of the naive factorization). Internal steps (`IPar`, with the
   `redexShell` constructor) reflect head-normality backward and merge with a
   following head step into a fresh parallel step, and the pullback runs on
   the lexicographic measure (head steps remaining, split index).

`Blc/Ratchet.lean` derives it a third time from loop32's certificate as
literal data.

## Layout

Core:

- `Blc/Term.lean` — de Bruijn terms (0-indexed; the wire format is our index
  + 1), one-pass β-substitution, closedness with decidability,
  shift/subst-on-closed lemmas.
- `Blc/Step.lean` — executable `headStep` mirroring the trusted Rust
  checker, the `HeadStep` relation, soundness/completeness/determinism,
  step-indexed `HeadReds`, `HeadDiverges`.
- `Blc/Subst.lean` — the five Nipkow–Berghofer shift/substitution equations
  for one-pass substitution.
- `Blc/Par.lean` — occurrence counting with its shift/subst interaction
  lemmas; `ParN` indexed parallel reduction; the substitution theorem at the
  exact index `n + occ j t' · m`.
- `Blc/Beta.lean` — full β-reduction, normal forms, the decidable syntactic
  normal-form predicate, the single-redex `Spine` discipline.
- `Blc/Factor.lean` — `IPar`, the indexed split, merge, the pullback, the
  general bridge; `parN_betas` (Par ⊆ Betas) and `headFactorizes_nf`, which
  preserves the identity of the chosen normal form.
- `Blc/Confluence.lean` — Church–Rosser via Takahashi complete development,
  preservation of weak normalization under finite β-reduction, and unique
  normal forms.
- `Blc/NormalOrder.lean` — productive normal-form descent: a finite
  head-normal landing exposes another family member strictly below binders
  or rigid applications, with a strictly smaller hypothetical normal form.
- `Blc/Wire.lean` — the kernel-computable BLC encoder behind every `wire_*`
  theorem (the emitters are untrusted; the kernel vouches for the decoding).

Certificate classes:

- `Blc/Loop32.lean` — A, W, C0, the tower, and loop32's ratchet (OPEN proved
  for all arguments; DESC and BASE lifted through right-spine contexts; the
  exact 2n+2 cycle arithmetic).
- `Blc/NoNf.lean` — tower/state invariant families; loop32 by route 1.
- `Blc/Sym.lean` — the symbolic checker layer: `STerm` (terms with opaque
  metavariables, mirroring the Rust checker's `PTerm`), grafting
  instantiation sound under closed environments, the executable `symHeadStep`
  (an opaque head aborts), and the one trusted rule, the commuting square
  `symHeadStep_sound`, transporting each symbolic step to a concrete
  `HeadStep` under every closed instantiation. `LiftReds`/`symStepsApp`
  package the proper-source-nonlam condition for lifting through application
  contexts.
- `Blc/Ratchet.lean` — the Ratchet assembly: `RatchetCert` (triple,
  obligation counts, INIT landing with under-binder and trailing-vector
  parts), `Valid` as seven decidable obligations packaged into one `decide`
  via `check`/`valid_of_check`, and the glue theorem ending in `HeadDiverges`
  and, through the bridge, `noNormalForm`.
- `Blc/HeadTower.lean` — the HeadTowerRatchet assembly: `HTRCert` with six
  obligations (BASE may be zero-step), the `OnlyMVar 0` wrapper gate (SPREAD
  instantiates two metavariable slots), the rank step as a literal seven-lemma
  lifted composition, and recursive descent costs; the quadratic closed form
  is never trusted glue.
- `Blc/Selector.lean` — the SelectorRatchet assembly: `SelCert` with
  FAN/SELECT obligations (`renameMVar01` and its instantiation lemma let
  SELECT mention `W[Q]` alongside `P[Z]`), constant-cost rank steps.
- `Blc/Passenger.lean` — the PassengerDiagonalRatchet assembly: `PdrCert`
  with OPEN/UNWRAP/DROP/SEED, the rank step and diagonal descent, and the
  exceptional base cycle.
- `Blc/Rigid.lean` — the rigid-head argument bridge for `*-ARG` kills:
  rigid-spine shape theory (a normal form of `λᵏ.(x a⃗)` forces normal forms
  of every spine argument, by elementary inversion), head-reduction
  determinism pinning the factorization's landing to the computed head normal
  form, and `argKill`: three decided shape premises plus the argument's
  certificate imply the outer term has no normal form.

Busy-beaver proofs (`docs/classical/certificates/busy-beaver.md`):

- `Blc/BB33First.lean` — universal head cycles with arbitrary open payloads,
  instantiated for one 33-bit and five 34-bit programs.
- `Blc/BB33Second.lean` — productive closed tower pairs.
- `Blc/BB34.lean` — two productive rigid-argument families.
- `Blc/BB34Compose.lean` — productive composition pairs with explicit
  open-variable shifts.
- `Blc/BB34Spine.lean` — a head-divergent family with growing spine arity.
- `Blc/BB35Pump.lean` — seven open-payload head pumps.
- `Blc/BB35Productive.lean` — two productive rigid families and two head
  pumps.
- `Blc/BB35Spine.lean` — four growing application-spine families.
- `Blc/BB35Reentry.lean` — the identity-wrapped reentry tower.
- `Blc/BB35Church.lean` — symbolic Church exponentiation and wire size,
  proving the 38,127,987,424,941-bit champion without expanding its normal
  form.
- `Blc/BB35Large.lean` — GENERATED by `tools/certificates/bb35_large.py`; a
  shared syntax graph proves the other large halter has 4,186,155,666 bits.

Self-interpretation:

- `Blc/Selfint.lean` — the pure fragment of `docs/quantum/bisimulation.md`:
  proves `intL`'s wire identity, `|E_q| = |intL I| = 174`, and the quote-size
  identity `|stream(M,R)| = 14·|M| + zeros(M) + |R|`; states the translation
  contract `Implements` and the parser obligations (`ParserStatement`, L1, L2)
  as named `Prop`s. It also pins the 165-bit interpreter `intL165` to its
  wire (`wire_intL165`); qBLC's `E_q` stays on `intL`.
- `Blc/SelfintProof.lean` — proves them, and the classical contract.
  `parse` is one induction on the source term (open or closed): the stream
  applied to the parser's handler spine-reduces (head reduction through
  application states only) to `K (λ.B) U` for every continuation `K`, where
  `λ.B` denotes `M` under β (`Den`: any cons' environment, under any number of
  binders, yields `M` instantiated) and implements it under head reduction
  (`Implements`), and the residual `U` reduces to the tail. The VAR engine is
  induction on the unary selector (`engine`), skipvar is `l l l → rest`
  (`skip`), and sixteen machine runs of the 168's control are kernel replays
  of head-step chains on placeholder terms, instantiated because head steps
  are stable under substitution. `Parses E` states the parse's conclusion for
  any interpreter `E`, and the corollaries derive from it alone
  (`*_of_parses`): `selfint_contract` (β-reduction for closed `M`, any `C`,
  `N`), `selfint_open`, `l1_beta`, `parserStatement`, `l1Statement`,
  `l2Statement`, and `isSelfInterpreter_intL` with `not_selfintSizeBound_169`.
- `Blc/Selfint165.lean` — the 165-bit interpreter: the 168 with the reader
  `\list. list (\bit0. bit0 (\unused \r. r next) cont) (list list list)`.
  Its third argument `s s s` is the stream past the first `0` (`pastZero`;
  for a variable it reduces through `skip_self`), and VAR returns it as the
  residual. `parse165` therefore quantifies over every third argument that
  spine-reduces there (`pastZero_self` covers APP's second parse, where it is
  `U₁ U₁ U₁` for an unreduced residual `U₁`). The closures, the VAR engine,
  and every run that does not name the parser are the 168's; eight runs are
  new. It proves `Parses intL165`, hence `selfint_contract165`,
  `selfint_open165`, `parserStatement165`, `isSelfInterpreter_intL165`, and
  `not_selfintSizeBound_166` (the upper-bound half of optimality; the lower
  bound `SelfintSizeBound 165` is stated, open).
- `Blc/SelfintLowerBound.lean` — every term with at most one syntactically
  nonlinear binder weakly normalizes. Consequently every self-interpreter
  has at least two, and an explicit `λc s.B` has two inside `B`. A syntax
  inequality gives `SelfintSizeBound 18`, without assuming a parser design.
- `Blc/SelfintCapture.lean` — absence of live capture of enclosing local
  variables is preserved by substitution and β-reduction. The interpreter
  contract forces such a capture in the interpreter's source.
- `Blc/SelfintOrigins.lean` — colored reduction tracks quotation-origin
  abstractions through every ordinary reduction. They remain affine, and
  live ones retain protected head shapes, no local capture, and normal
  bodies containing only quotation-origin abstractions. A red-abstraction
  accounting theorem proves that every interpreter must duplicate
  interpreter-origin code along every successful trace for a suitable
  closed normal source.
- `Blc/SelfintNormal.lean` — the 204-bit β-normal interpreter, including its
  exact wire and directed contract for every closed source and arbitrary
  continuation and suffix.
- `Blc/SelfintSharp.lean` — a sound checker for explicit contextual β traces;
  a 584-bit interpreter with exactly two nonlinear binders, both closed in
  isolation; and a 742-bit β-normal interpreter with the same property.
  The first reduces to the literal 165; the second has a β-joinability
  contract. Thus the nonlinear count is sharp even among normal interpreters.
- `Blc/SelfintTyped.lean` — the 672-bit β-normal, simply typed interpreter.
  A proved annotation checker supplies an ordinary STLC judgment. A finite
  cast-erasure trace transfers the 165's directed contract. Its applications
  to quotations need not be simply typable.
- `Blc/SelfintControl.lean` — arbitrary continuation/suffix instances of the
  β-joinability contract and the unconditional theorem that `E E` cannot
  be β-equal to an exact self-interpreter `E`.

Generated:

- `Certs/` — GENERATED by `blam cert lean`; one module per term size.
- `BusyBeaver.lean` — GENERATED by `tools/certificates/bb.py`.

## Build

Lean 4.32.2 via elan, no mathlib.

```bash
cd lean && lake build                    # the default target, Blc
cd lean && lake build Certs BusyBeaver   # both certificate registries
```

The default target is `Blc` (the root `Blc.lean`), which does not import the
PDR, confluence, or BB modules; `lake build Certs BusyBeaver` builds those
through their importers and kernel-checks every certificate. Discovery stays
outside the formal surface. Formalizing prefix-freeness and Kraft accounting
from `Blc/Wire.lean`, and with it machine-checked K upper bounds, is on the
classical docket (`docs/STATUS.md`).
