No sub-165 interpreter found. No enumeration was performed. Two complete
hand-derived interpreters were constructed and tested, with two battery
workers. The repository was not modified. The 165 record was read first;
the constructions below repair the recorded failing r intL idea rather than
propose that known failure as a candidate. Their purpose is to determine
whether the reader's duplicated second-bit stream can supply useful work.

The 225 uses the zero arm (lambda r.r next), deliberately retaining the
extra application that makes the recorded 162-bit near-miss fail. For a
constructor, the reader now calls next b t r, where r = cons b t is the
duplicated stream beginning with the second tag bit. Thus r P = P b t.
This yields the first parsed value in ABS, and its remainder in APP.
A complementary parse obtains the missing component. The 234 computes
the complementary component once with P (b F K) t and binds it as other;
the extra selector, let, and binder depths make this nine bits larger.

These are not proposed improvements. Relative to 165, the 225 has four
more lambdas, seven more applications, and 24 more index-excess bits:
8 + 28 + 24 = 60. The experiment identifies a correct use of the extra
reader argument, but obtaining its missing information is too expensive
in these constructions. No minimality of these repairs is claimed.

EXACT IDENTITIES AND CORRECTNESS PROOF

Notation: K = lambda x y.x (bit 0); F = lambda x y.y (bit 1);
delta(s) = s s s; cell(a,e) = lambda x y.x a (y e).
Write A(f) = lambda e a.f(cell(a,e)), B(f,g) = lambda e.f e (g e).
P is a parser with the usual value/remainder interface, including on open
syntax. Its recursive syntactic value V(M,R) is:

  V(var n,R) = the stream 1^(n-1)0 ++ R;
  V(lambda M,R) = A(V(M,R));
  V(M N,R) = B(V(M,quote(N)++R), V(N,R)).

The standard property is P k (quote(M)++R) =beta k V(M,R) R.

1. Selected parsing from the duplicated stream.

For r = cons b (quote(M)++R), set f=V(M,R). Then

  r P =beta P b (quote(M)++R) =beta b f R.

Consequently r P =beta f when b=K and =beta R when b=F.
The complementary call P (b F K) (quote(M)++R) is R in the
first case and f in the second. Proof: expand the Scott cons, use the
parser law, and reduce K/F. This identity does not evaluate f.

2. The 225's constructor law.

The outer reader is s (lambda b0.b0 (lambda r.r next) c) delta(s).
For first bit 0, s=cons K r and delta(s)=r, so this reduces to
r next r = next b t r. Its next body is exactly

  b [c (A(r P)) (P F t)]
    [P (lambda g.c (B(P K t,g))) (r P)].

When b=K and t=quote(M)++R, identity 1 makes this c (A(f)) R.
When b=F and t=quote(M)++quote(N)++R, identity 1 supplies the second
operand's input stream, P K t supplies f, and the second parse produces
c (B(f,g)) R. The variable case is unchanged: first bit F selects c,
and the Scott stream supplies the unary value and delta(s)=R.

This is an induction on source syntax. Every recursive parse used by a
constructor parses a proper subterm; duplicating a parse does not change
that induction measure. It proves precisely the standard P k law above,
with the same V as the 165. Therefore the existing environment-denotation
argument yields the exact closed-program contract, divergent syntax
included. This is a paper proof, not a new Lean formalization.

For the 234, name other = P (b F K) t. Its two branches are
c (A(r P)) other and P (lambda g.c (B(other,g))) (r P).
Identity 1 gives the same induction immediately.

INTERFACE IMPOSSIBILITY RESULTS

3. Selecting just one parse component cannot be repaired downstream
without retaining input-dependent information about the discarded one.

Assume a fixed postprocessor receives b and x=P b (quote(M)++R),
along with c and P, but has no other dependence on the input stream.
For ABS, choose the closed body I and two distinct rigid final tails.
x=P K (quote(I)++R)=beta K I is independent of R, but the required
outputs c (A(K I)) R have different rigid tails. Church-Rosser rules out
a common result. For APP, use first operands I and K, the same second
operand I, and the same final tail. Both selected inputs equal
quote(I)++R, but the required results contain I versus K I, distinct
beta-normal forms. Thus no such postprocessor exists at any size.

This proves an obstruction for the already-recorded r P failure. It does
not prohibit retaining r (as the two constructions do), retaining the
first value, or changing the value/remainder protocol.

4. Supplying the skipped original stream as well still loses variable
indices in APP. This also holds for every positive iterate of skip.

Assume the ordinary open-term parser law. Let the first APP operand be
var n, let R be the second-operand stream, and let

  t_n = 1^n 0 ++ R,       r_n = 1^(n+1) 0 ++ R.

The available selected result is P F t_n =beta R. Also
delta(r_n)=beta R by the unary skip law, so for every j>=1,
delta^j(r_n)=beta delta^(j-1)(R). All these fields are independent of n.
Hence a postprocessor whose only stream-dependent inputs are the
selected result and any collection of these positive skipped residuals
cannot recover which first variable was parsed.

This is a contradiction within the closed-program contract, not merely
an open-term disagreement: place the APP under two lambdas and let its
second operand be var 1. The two closed programs are

  lambda a b.b b       and       lambda a b.a b.

At the APP boundary their fixed preceding context is identical and all
the proposed fields coincide, but their required values have different
beta-normal forms. Their BLC normal forms are 0000011010 and
00000111010. targeted_checks.json records a normalizer check that both
the selected fields and delta(r_n) coincide for n=1,2; the general proof
is the unary reduction above. The result applies only to the stated
interface. A different VAR return, an unskipped pointer, or an extra
index-bearing field escapes it.

READ OF THE REMAINING OPPORTUNITY

The extra zero-branch argument can carry useful syntax/control state;
it is not intrinsically disposable. But using its native r P projection
splits the exact pair the constructor needs. Combining that projection
with one or more skipped pointers does not restore the missing value
when APP's first operand is a variable. Reparsing restores it, at the
measured cost above.

A smaller design along this route would have to preserve the raw unary
suffix or another index-bearing value while using the stream's own
application to supply the continuation arguments. Merely moving skip
later or taking more skipped residuals cannot do that in this interface.
I do not have a lower-cost carrier satisfying those requirements, nor a
lower bound ruling one out. This leaves the sub-165 problem open.
