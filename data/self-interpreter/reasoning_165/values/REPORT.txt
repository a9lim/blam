NOTATION

K = lambda x y. x, F = lambda x y. y, I = lambda x. x.
cell(a,e) = lambda b r. b a (r e).
X_1^R = cons K R; X_(n+1)^R = cons F (X_n^R).
Thus X_n^R is precisely the current raw value for variable n.

1. AN ALL-SIZE OBSTRUCTION TO THE CELL/APP ALIAS

For every environment term e, n >= 1 and suffix R:

    e (K I) X_n^R =beta e F X_n^R =beta X_(n+1)^R e.

The first equality is K I =beta F. The second is the definition of cons.
Consequently the proposed lift

    lift_alias f g = lambda e. e f g

satisfies the exact identity

    lift_alias (K I) X_n^R =beta X_(n+1)^R.

The final equality is beta equality, not an eta assumption: X_(n+1)^R is
literally lambda h. h F X_n^R, so expanding it establishes the equality.

If the recursive parser has the public raw-value ABI, its closed I result
must be K I =beta F, for every suffix. Therefore this lift compiles I (var n)
as var (n+1). In particular the two closed source terms

    lambda a b. I b
    lambda a b. a

receive beta-equal body values and therefore beta-equal outputs through any
common ABS constructor of the current interface. Their required outputs are
C (lambda z a b. b) N and C (lambda z a b. a) N, distinct beta normal forms.
Church-Rosser gives a contradiction.

This removes the size cap on repairing that particular APP alias by changing
only the environment cell. It assumes bare-variable values, canonical closed
values, and the current ABS interface. A new internal parser ABI or a public
decoder can escape those assumptions; it is not an unrestricted lower bound.
The core identity was also checked with rigid e and R by Python and Rust.

2. THE MODE-FREE BUILDER OBSTRUCTION DOES NOT DEPEND ON MARKER K

Suppose W must satisfy, for some arbitrary ABS marker q0,

    W f q0 e =beta lambda a. f (cell(a,e))
    W f g  e =beta f e (g e)                 for all raw parsed values g.

Take f0 = K (K I), the raw closure of lambda x. I, and g = K Omega, the raw
closure of the closed divergent program Omega. Set e = I. The APP equation
requires

    W f0 (K Omega) I =beta I.

K Omega has no head normal form: its initial lambda does not make it
solvable. By the standard genericity lemma, since the displayed context has
normal form I, replacing that entire unsolvable argument by any q gives the
same normal form I. In particular W f0 q0 I =beta I. But the ABS equation
requires

    W f0 q0 I =beta lambda a. K I = K (K I),

a different normal form. This contradicts Church-Rosser. The marker can even
depend on f/e: instantiate it after choosing f0 and I. Changing K to I,
another normal marker, or another unsolvable marker does not help. Nor does
merely eta-expanding the divergent raw closure.

This is a handwritten proof using the standard genericity lemma already
used in the repository record. It is not newly kernel checked.

The exact escape is to make the discriminated object solvable independently
of its value. Define

    None = K
    Some(g) = lambda none some. some g
    W f q e = q (lambda a. f (cell(a,e)))
                  (lambda g. f e (g e)).

Then W f None e and W f (Some(g)) e have the desired ABS/APP reductions.
Some(K Omega) has head normal form lambda none some. some (K Omega), so the
genericity argument cannot replace it by an arbitrary marker. It does not
have a full normal form; head solvability is the relevant distinction.

The 203 implements precisely this escape. For b = K its optional parser
lambda k. k b returns None without consuming the stream. For b = F it invokes
the ordinary parser and returns Some(g). The outer value continuation is
lambda q. cont (lambda e. W exp q e). Substituting either tag reduces NEXT
to the original ABS or APP continuation with the same remaining stream.
Both equalities were verified with rigid p, cont and operand parameters in
Python/Rust. Together with the unchanged reader, the usual structural parser
induction proves the contract, including divergent source terms: the parser
never needs to normalize a constructed operand value.

3. A SUFFIX-INDEPENDENT BOOLEAN / SCOTT-BIT-CELL DISCRIMINATOR

    T = lambda t. t K K K I

satisfies

    T K =beta K
    T F =beta K
    T (cons b r) =beta F      for b in {K,F}, arbitrary r.

Proof: the first two applications reduce to K K I = K. For a cell:

    cons b r K K K I
      -> K b r K K I
      -> b K K I
      -> K I
      -> F.

No reduction in r is used. Python/Rust checks include rigid tails and Omega
tails for both bit choices. This is a genuine type separation available on
the promised input domains; it is not a discriminator for arbitrary parsed
closures, which can be unsolvable or equal F.

bit_or_cell.lam is 37 bits. Sharing K explicitly gives 36 bits:

    (lambda k t. t k k k I) K

in bit_or_cell_shared.lam. These are helper terms, not self-interpreters.

A small conditional lower bound explains the cost. In the unshared template
lambda t. t K A B C with closed A,B,C and these exact K/F outputs, the K input
forces B =beta K. The one-cell input headed by F forces B C =beta F, hence
C =beta I. The other equations require A I =beta F and A K I =beta K. Neither
closed term below 7 bits (I or F) meets them, so |A| >= 7; also |B| >= 7 and
|C| >= 4. Including the first K and syntax gives at least 37 bits, achieved
by A=B=K,C=I. The 36-bit shared form deliberately leaves that template. This
is not a lower bound for arbitrary classifiers or for an interpreter reader.

4. ABS CONSTRUCTOR AS A RECURSION DRIVER

Let

    A f e a = f (cell(a,e))
    delta = lambda h. h h
    B c p = the 165 reader/NEXT body with cont=c, intL=p,
            and its ABS value written A exp
    H = A (lambda env. env B delta)
    E = delta H.

Then the following is an exact finite beta reduction:

    E c = H H c
        -> (lambda env. env B delta) (cell(c,H))
        -> cell(c,H) B delta
        -> B c (delta H)
         = B c E.

The check counted seven head beta contractions with B rigid and found the
expected AST exactly. This makes the environment cell supply both the public
continuation and the recursive parser. The same A is used by genuine ABS
nodes, so the helper is shared rather than copied into the driver.

There is also a direct proof transferring the 165's full contract. Removing
the outer A let from abs_driver.lam leaves delta H. Expanding the finite
generator H and expanding the 165's finite knot generator give identical
beta normal forms. Both Python and Rust checked that equality; the common
bits are in common_generator_nf.blc. By congruence, the two entire closed
interpreters are beta-convertible. This construction adds no new semantics
and is too large at 193 bits.

WHERE A SUB-165 COULD STILL HIDE

The cheap APP alias has an all-size collision, not merely a small-cell
failure. A marker substitution cannot rescue a tagless ABS/APP builder. These
two observations make the current raw-value interface an especially narrow
place to seek another local saving.

A genuinely different internal representation can evade both arguments.
It must retain a visible control head even when its semantic payload is
Omega, or alter which object the operation discriminates. A surrounding
Scott continuation/option has that property. If the same E is recursively
called with its public interface, however, every closed subprogram still
returns K M; an internally tagged representation needs a distinct entry or
a control carrier that the public continuation never receives as its value.

The concrete remaining prospect is to obtain that carrier from applications
already present in the reader or recursion driver, so packing/decoding is
not paid separately. The boolean/cell identity is a possible ingredient if
its K arguments are already supplied by control flow. Its standalone cost
is far larger than the four-bit APP-lift saving. The 203 shows that allocating
the carrier explicitly does not pay, and the 193 shows that explicitly
sharing the current ABS constructor with the knot does not pay either.

I have no sub-165 candidate, no unconditional interpreter lower bound, and
no justified new local enumeration to recommend. The surviving direction
is a control/value fusion that supplies a solvable carrier implicitly;
this remains a hypothesis, not a construction.
