   R(q,c) Z_t
   -> Z_t H(q,c) (Z_t Z_t Z_t)
   -> T_t T_t
   -> t t.

   The middle step uses Z_t H -> T_t and
   Z_t Z_t Z_t -> T_t Z_t -> Z_t t -> T_t.
   Neither q nor c is evaluated. In fact the first argument H(q,c) can be
   replaced by any A(s): (lambda s.s A(s) (s s s)) Z_t ->* t t, because
   Z_t discards it. This is stronger than the special t=q case.
   A Rust normal-form check with q,c,t bound rigidly passed.

3. For any Scott cell s=cons b r, H(q,c) s ->* r q c.

WHY THE RAW PARSER/NEXT CANNOT BE THE CARRIER

Take any term with the exact reader shape
  P = lambda c s.s H_c (s s s),
where H_c is arbitrary and may depend on c. Put T=P P P. Then

  T ->* P H_P T ->* T H_(H_P) (T T T).

These are head reductions and the recursive copy of T is again at the head.
Repeating them gives an infinite head-reduction sequence, with two more spine
arguments each time. By head standardization T has no head normal form.

Furthermore, for any fixed R_i,
  R_i P -> P H_i (P P P)
        -> T H_(H_i) (T T T),
so R_i P also has no head normal form.

This rules out feeding a full parser directly into the reader to replace the
encoded carrier Z_t. It also rules out feeding the 165's current NEXT directly:
after unfolding E, q_c has this same two-lambda reader shape, with its first
parameter the tag bit. The result is conditional on this exact reader/skip
interface, not a lower bound for arbitrary interpreters.

WHY THE CURRENT NEXT DIAGONAL DOES NOT REPLACE E c

Let q=q_c. Define
  k(f) = q (c A(f)) (E a(c,f)).
Then q q = E k.

For a fixed f, set
  d_f = lambda g.c A(f) (c A(g)) (E a(c,g)).
Expanding q gives
  k(f) = E d_f (E a(c,f)).

Apply identity 5 with i corresponding to d_f and j to a(c,f). With
q_d=Q(E,d_f), H_d=H(q_d,d_f), R_a=E a(c,f), this yields

  k(f) ->*
    c V(f,q_d) d_f (d_f q_d d_f H_d) (R_a R_a R_a).

On quote(I) ++ N, the correct first parse supplies v=lambda z.I, so

  q q (quote(I) ++ N) ->*
    c V(v,q_d) d_v (d_v q_d d_v H_d) (R_a R_a R_a) N.

There are FIVE arguments at the rigid c head, whereas the required c v N has
TWO. Beta-reduction cannot remove arguments from that rigid head. Thus q q
cannot replace E c under the fixed NEXT protocol.

A closed observational check sets c=lambda a b c d e.e. With N bound rigidly,
  lambda N.q q (quote(I) ++ N) normalizes to 0010         (lambda N.N),
  lambda N.E c (quote(I) ++ N) normalizes to 0000000010   (lambda N a b c.c).
Both normal forms were checked by the Rust machine.

ROOT-NEXT CONSTRUCTION

The 214 uses a continuation-last NEXT Q and the shared parser
  P_Q c s = s (lambda b.b (lambda u r.r Q c) c) (s s s).
Its fixed-point equation is
  Q b s c = P_Q (lambda f.b (c A(f)) (P_Q a(c,f))) s.
The root is
  E_root c s = s (lambda u r.r Q) c.
Every closed source begins with a zero tag. For s=cons K r, both E_root c s
and P_Q c s reduce to r Q c. The same syntax induction proves P_Q correct.
The boundary shortcut is sound, but the continuation-last plumbing and binding
depth dominate its savings.

WHERE A SMALLER TERM COULD HIDE

The new positive seam is the unrestricted R_i Z_t -> t t identity. It avoids
the handler-only C-headed exclusion by mixing the reader and the zero-arm
constructor. The 192 realizes this seam without a separate syntactic knot.

To improve 165, the encoded-generator adapter would have to share a real
semantic role. The natural candidate is to make the existing Z_next also be
Z_generator, so that NEXT carries its own recursive generator. The raw parser
and current NEXT are excluded as direct carriers by the head-divergence result,
and current NEXT self-application is excluded by the rigid-head arity proof.
Such a design must therefore change NEXT's behavior on non-Boolean arguments
and how continuations are carried, while retaining its two Boolean cases.
That is an open construction problem here, not a claim of a near solution.

No global or sub-165 optimality conclusion follows from this consult.
