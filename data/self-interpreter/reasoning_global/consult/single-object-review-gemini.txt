**1. Counterexample search (Proposition & Theorem R) — Verdict: Holds (no counterexample).**
- *Dead abstractions holding two terms* (`Z = λu. D`, `u ∉ FV(D)`): `(λu. D) M →w D` ignores `M`, so `A` and `B` cannot branch; spine copies of `Z` reconstruct from `D` via `λu. ζ'`.
- *Nonlinear sharing / unevaluated redexes* (e.g. `λu. u R R`, `R = (λz. x) y`): internal β in one copy breaks `SO`, but `R` only yields `x`. Extracting `x` from one copy of `R` and `y` from another (`Q' = u (r K) (r F)`) requires `R` itself (`SO(R)`, `FV(R) ⊆ {x, y}`) to violate the Proposition.
- *Capture tricks or non-`SO` templates `Q'`* (e.g. `Q' = λw. w (u r)`): `Q = Q'[R/r]` is capture-avoiding, so `Q'`'s binders cannot capture `FV(R)` differently across sites; `Q'` need not be `SO` because (c2) moves `Q'` into the unrestricted context `G'`.
- *Context-supplied pairing / self-application* (`G` passing `P` to `Z = λv. B`): if `v` is live, `B = B'[R₀/r₀]` with `v ∉ FV(R₀)` forces `x, y` into a `v`-free `SO` term `R₀` before `v` can combine them.
- *Application splits* (`Z = M N`, `x ∈ FV(M)`, `y ∈ FV(N)`): either `M` reduces to `x` leaving `N` stuck (`x N … ≠ x, y`), or `M` is an abstraction and `Z →w Z₁` stays `SO` by L2.
